About the Film

This documentary follows Katie Senft, a research faculty member in the biology department at the University of Nevada, Reno, and the captain of the university's research vessel at Lake Tahoe. Out on the water, she and her colleagues study microplastics and other particles that affect the health and clarity of the lake.

The Science Behind the Story

The research team collects lake water at three depths: the surface, 5 meters, and 15 meters. The water is pumped through a stack of sieves that catch microplastics of different sizes, and the samples are then taken to the lab for testing. Their sampling line runs from near the shore by Incline Creek out to the middle of the lake, which shows how plastics are spread across the water.

To measure what is in the water, the team lowers a device called a LISST 200X that records the size and count of particles at different depths. They are also adding a newer instrument, the CytoSub, which can analyze particles as small as 0.1 micrometers and reads how particles interact with light. That helps tell different kinds of particles apart, including very small plankton.

Microplastics are not the only thing affecting how clear Tahoe is. Too much phosphorus and nitrogen can act like fertilizer and fuel larger algae blooms. The project is funded by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and is a joint effort with the Desert Research Institute.

The People

The film centers on Katie Senft and the research team working alongside her on Lake Tahoe.

  • Katie Senft: research faculty in the biology department and captain of the research vessel.
  • Carina Seitz: research assistant professor in biology and part of the Tahoe Institute for Global Sustainability. She earned her Ph.D. in geology in Argentina and now analyzes Lake Tahoe's clarity and microplastics.
  • Consuelo Del Rio: biology student on the research team.
  • Sudeep Chandra, Ph.D.: professor of limnology and faculty member of the Tahoe Institute for Global Sustainability, who works with Seitz on the project.
